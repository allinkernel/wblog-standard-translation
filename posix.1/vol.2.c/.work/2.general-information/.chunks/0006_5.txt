[p_00792] Each function that operates on a stream is said to have zero or more &quot;underlying functions&quot;. This means that the

implementations of the stream function and its underlying functions.
[h4_00793] [A748][/A748]2.5.2 Stream Orientation and Encoding Rules
[p_00794] The definition of a stream includes an &quot;orientation&quot;. After a stream is associated with an external file, but
before any operations are performed on it, the stream is without orientation. Once a wide-character input/output function has been
applied to a stream without orientation, the stream shall become &quot;wide-oriented&quot;. Similarly, once a byte input/output function
has been applied to a stream without orientation, the stream shall become &quot;byte-oriented&quot;. Only a call to the [A749][I750]freopen[/I750]()[/A749] function or the [A751][I752]fwide[/I752]()[/A751] function can
otherwise alter the orientation of a stream.
[p_00795] A successful call to [A753][I754]freopen[/I754]()[/A753] shall remove any orientation. The
three predefined streams [I755]standard input[/I755], [I756]standard output[/I756], and [I757]standard error[/I757] shall be unoriented at program
start-up.
[p_00796] Byte input/output functions cannot be applied to a wide-oriented stream, and wide-character input/output functions
cannot be applied to a byte-oriented stream. The remaining stream operations shall not affect and shall not be affected by a
stream's orientation, except for the following additional restriction:
[li_00797] For wide-oriented streams, after a successful call to a file-positioning function that leaves the file position
indicator prior to the end-of-file, a wide-character output function can overwrite a partial character; any file contents beyond
the byte(s) written are henceforth undefined.
